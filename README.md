Introduction and Methodology This report details the process and findings of a sentiment analysis conducted on employee messages, aiming to understand communication patterns, identify key individuals, and assess potential flight risk. Approach: The analysis followed a multi-stage approach:

Data Loading and Preparation: The employee message data was loaded from an Excel file into a pandas DataFrame.

Sentiment Analysis: The VADER (Valence Aware Dictionary and sEntiment Reasoner) library was used to assign a sentiment label (Positive, Negative, or Neutral) to each message based on its textual content. VADER is a lexicon and rule-based sentiment analysis tool specifically attuned to sentiments expressed in social media, which makes it suitable for analyzing informal communication like emails.

Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA): A thorough examination of the data structure, sentiment distribution, and trends over time was performed.

Employee Scoring and Ranking: Monthly sentiment scores were calculated for each employee, and employees were ranked based on these scores.

Flight Risk Identification: Employees were flagged as at-risk based on a specific criterion related to the frequency of negative messages.

Predictive Modeling (Conceptual Overview): While a full predictive model was not built within this analysis, the potential for using this data for predictive modeling is discussed. Methodology: • Sentiment Labeling: VADER's SentimentIntensityAnalyzer was used to compute a compound sentiment score for each message body. A threshold of 0.05 for positive and -0.05 for negative was applied to classify messages as Positive, Negative, or Neutral. • Employee Scoring: A numerical score (+1 for Positive, -1 for Negative, 0 for Neutral) was assigned to each message. These scores were then summed monthly for each employee, with the sum resetting at the beginning of each month. • Flight Risk Identification: A rolling 30-day window was used to count the number of negative emails sent by each employee. Employees with 4 or more negative emails within any rolling 30-day period were identified as at risk.

Employee Scoring and Ranking Scoring Process: A monthly sentiment score was calculated for each employee. Each message was assigned a score based on its sentiment: +1 for Positive, -1 for Negative, and 0 for Neutral. These scores were then aggregated by summing them up for each employee within each calendar month. The score for each employee was reset at the beginning of every new month. Ranking Process: Based on the monthly sentiment scores, employees were ranked to identify those with the highest positive and lowest negative sentiment in each month. • Top Three Positive Employees: The three employees with the highest positive monthly sentiment scores were identified. The ranking was determined first by the sentiment score (descending) and then by alphabetical order of their email address (ascending) in case of ties. • Top Three Negative Employees: The three employees with the lowest (most negative) monthly sentiment scores were identified using the same sorting criteria as the positive ranking (score ascending, then alphabetical ascending). Flight Risk Identification Criteria: Employees were identified as being at risk of leaving if they sent 4 or more negative emails within a rolling 30-day period. This criterion focuses on the frequency of negative communication within a recent timeframe, irrespective of the overall monthly sentiment score. A rolling 30-day count ensures that the analysis captures recent negative trends, even if spread across month boundaries. Overview and Evaluation of the Predictive Model While a formal predictive model for flight risk was not built as part of this analysis, the data and insights gained provide a strong foundation for developing such a model. Potential Model Approach: A predictive model could utilize features derived from this sentiment analysis and other potential data sources (e.g., HR data, activity logs) to predict the likelihood of an employee leaving. Potential features could include: • Monthly sentiment scores and trends • Frequency of negative messages in rolling time windows • Changes in communication patterns • Interaction patterns with other employees Machine learning algorithms such as logistic regression, random forests, or time series models could be explored for this purpose. Evaluation: The evaluation of such a model would require a labeled dataset of employees who have left the organization. Standard classification metrics such as precision, recall, F1-score, and AUC would be used to assess the model's performance in identifying at-risk employees. Limitations and Future Work: The current analysis is based solely on email sentiment. A more comprehensive predictive model would benefit from incorporating other relevant data sources and considering external factors that might influence employee retention. Further research into the specific content of negative messages could also provide valuable context for understanding the root causes of dissatisfaction.

Conclusion The sentiment analysis and EDA provided valuable insights into employee communication patterns and sentiment within the organization. The identification of employees at risk based on the frequency of negative messages highlights potential areas for proactive intervention. The findings from this analysis can serve as a basis for further investigation and the development of more sophisticated predictive models to support employee retention efforts.
